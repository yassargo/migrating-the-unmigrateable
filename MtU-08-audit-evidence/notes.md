**Auditors don't fail organizations for having gaps. They fail organizations for not knowing they have gaps — and for not being able to show what they're doing about the ones they know about.**

Three audiences will ask about your OT security posture. They want different things.

**NIS2 Competent Authorities** want evidence of a systematic approach. A documented program with gaps you're actively closing beats no program at all. Technical staff are increasingly present in inspections. Follow-up questions will be asked.

**Cyber Insurance Underwriters** ask at every renewal. They want specific, verifiable answers. Not narrative. Evidence.

**Internal Audit and Boards** want a coherent narrative. Is the risk understood? Is there a plan? Is it progressing? 

Fifteen questions show up consistently. The ones that catch organizations aren't the primary questions but rather follow-ups:

_"Show me the last three months of your vulnerability log."_  
_"Walk me through the last change you made to your firewall rules."_  

**Here's what the difference looks like:**

Remote access: ❌ "We use VPN" → ✅ Register of every path, MFA confirmed, logging demonstrated, quarterly review record, example of access revoked when a vendor contract ended.

EOL devices: ❌ "We're working on a replacement plan" → ✅ Compensating controls register — each device with VLAN isolation, protocol whitelist, signed risk acceptance, committed replacement date.

Incident response: ❌ "We'd call IT" → ✅ OT-specific plan, named roles, OT scenarios, tested within 12 months, lessons learned applied.

The question auditors are really asking isn't _"are you secure?"_ It's _"are you managing this?"_ Those are completely different questions.
